Motivation

Can chest X-rays and their reports tell us how an admission will be coded?

Why ICD codes

They summarise every hospital stay and drive billing, statistics and research cohorts.

How they're made

Coders read the whole record after discharge: about 19 codes per admission here.

Why chest X-rays

Among the most common data in an admission: images show findings, reports describe them.

Research questions
Q1

How well can chest X-ray images and reports predict an admission's complete code set?

Q2

Does combining image and text beat either one alone?

Q3

Does a contrastive loss that knows which admissions have similar codes help?

Q4

What limits performance: the model, or the information in the inputs?

Conclusions

Four questions, four answers

Q1 · how well?
+57%

micro AP over the baseline: 0.2205 vs 0.1408; P@5 0.44 vs 0.36. Best: 0.2227 with fine-tuning.

Q2 · fusion?
+0.03 AP

over the best single modality. Image and text alone are about equal (0.19, 0.18).

Q3 · contrastive loss?
+0.0025

with Jaccard SupCon, on 3 of 3 seeds. Hierarchy-aware weights add nothing.

Q4 · what limits it?
≈ 7%

of positive labels fall in a hand-picked set of chest-visible diagnoses. The inputs, not the model, set the ceiling.

Limitations
  • 22% of admissions dropped (no local image)
  • Only chest X-rays and reports, not notes, labs or vitals
Next steps
  • Predict at the ICD category level (e.g. 428 heart failure), where the X-ray carries the signal
  • Add notes, labs and vitals
